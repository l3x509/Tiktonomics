TikTok does not pay per individual view. It pays per 1,000 qualified views through its Creator Rewards Program — and in 2026, that rate is significantly higher than most creators expect based on what they read about the old Creator Fund.

⚡ Verified Answer — 2026

TikTok pays approximately $0.40 to $1.00 per 1,000 qualified views through the Creator Rewards Program. High-CPM niches like finance and tech regularly see $1.20+. A video with 1 million views earns most creators $400 to $1,000. This is verified by multiple creator reports and multiple independent 2026 sources cross-referencing the same RPM ranges.

Creator Rewards Program vs the Old Creator Fund

If you've seen headlines about TikTok paying creators almost nothing per view, that was the old Creator Fund — a program launched in 2020 that paid a fixed pool divided among all creators. TikTok shut down the Creator Fund in the US, UK, Germany, and France in late 2023 and replaced it with the Creator Rewards Program, which works completely differently.

Who Qualifies for Creator Rewards in 2026

The Creator Rewards Program has stricter requirements than the old Creator Fund, which is partly why it pays more:

  • Age: 18 or older
  • Followers: 10,000 minimum
  • Views: 100,000 video views in the last 30 days
  • Video length: Videos must be at least 60 seconds long
  • Content: Original content only — not Duets, Stitches, or reposts
  • Account standing: No major Community Guidelines violations
  • Eligible country: United States, United Kingdom, Germany, France, Japan, South Korea, Mexico, or Brazil
💡 The 100K views requirement is where most fail

Most creators who hit 10,000 followers get stuck on the 100,000 views in 30 days requirement. This isn't a lifetime number — it resets every 30 days. You need consistent monthly performance, not just one viral video from six months ago.

What TikTok Counts as a Qualified View

Not every view on your video counts toward your Creator Rewards earnings. TikTok filters views before paying you, and understanding what gets filtered is key to understanding your actual payout.

Views that do not count as qualified:

  • Views shorter than approximately 5 seconds (low-retention exits)
  • Views generated from paid promotions (TikTok Promote)
  • Views from bots or suspicious accounts
  • Views from accounts that don't reach the For You feed (the video must receive at least 1,000 organic For You feed views to qualify)
  • Duet and Stitch views don't qualify the original creator in some cases

In practice, most creators report their qualified view count being roughly 60–85% of total views for organic content. A video with 1 million total views might have 700,000–850,000 qualified views, and it's the qualified number that TikTok uses to calculate your RPM payout.

What Makes Your RPM Go Up or Down

Your RPM — revenue per 1,000 views — is not fixed. It varies by video, by week, and by the factors TikTok weighs when assigning ad revenue to your content.

Factors that increase your RPM

  • Watch time: Videos where viewers watch all the way through earn significantly more. A 2-minute video watched 100% outearns a 2-minute video abandoned at 30 seconds with the same view count.
  • Audience location: US, UK, and German viewers generate higher advertiser CPMs than most other regions. If most of your viewers are American, your RPM will be higher than a creator with the same view count but a Southeast Asian audience.
  • Niche: Advertisers pay premium prices to appear next to finance, business, tech, and health content. They pay less for entertainment and general content.
  • Engagement: Likes, comments, shares, and saves signal content quality to the algorithm, which affects both reach and RPM.
  • Originality score: TikTok's system measures how unique your content is. Heavily trending formats or near-duplicates of other videos score lower.

Factors that decrease your RPM

  • Low watch-through rate (viewers leaving in the first few seconds)
  • Content that receives a content advisory or label
  • Videos that don't reach the minimum 1,000 For You feed views per video to qualify
  • Audience skewed toward lower-CPM countries
  • Repurposed content from other platforms detected as non-original

Estimated TikTok RPM by Content Niche (2026)

The difference is advertiser demand: a financial services brand pays more per impression than a consumer goods brand.

The Bottom Line on TikTok Pay Per View in 2026

TikTok's Creator Rewards Program is a genuine improvement over the old Creator Fund — $0.40–$1.00 per 1,000 qualified views is a rate that can contribute meaningfully to a creator's income at scale. A creator consistently hitting 5 million views per month in a mid-CPM niche can realistically earn $2,500–$4,500 per month from views alone, before adding any other income stream.

The key insight is that not every view qualifies, niche matters enormously for RPM, and per-view income alone is rarely enough without stacking LIVE gifts, brand deals, or affiliate commissions on top.
